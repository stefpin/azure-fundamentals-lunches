🏪 Meanwhile at Nusantara Retail · Episode 03

The business case from last week landed well. Ibu Sari now asks the practical follow-up: "If we move the shop to Azure, where does it actually live? Jakarta? Singapore? And what happens when that building has a bad day, like our pantry room did when the roof leaked?"

Everyone has an opinion. Dewi wants the closest region because the checkout page feels slow on mobile networks. The finance team has heard that the same service can cost a different amount in another country. Pak Budi points out that the customer database holds names, phone numbers and delivery addresses of Indonesian shoppers, and the legal team would like that data to stay in Indonesia.

You open the list of Azure regions and find dozens of names, zone counts, pairs and footnotes. Before anyone creates a production resource, you need a clear answer for the director: which region should Nusantara Retail use, and how does the shop survive a failure in a building, or in the whole region?

Reading

Minutes 5–30 · 5 scenes
Scene 1 of 5

From a building to a geography

Azure is not one giant computer in one place. It is a worldwide fleet of datacenters: buildings full of servers, storage and network equipment, each with its own power feeds, cooling and physical security. You never pick a datacenter directly. Azure groups datacenters into larger units, and you choose among those units instead.

A region is a set of datacenters in one metropolitan area, connected by a fast, low-latency network. When you create a virtual machine, a storage account or a database, you almost always choose a region, and the resource lives there. Each region has a display name for people and a programmatic name for scripts: Indonesia Central is indonesiacentral, Southeast Asia is southeastasia. Microsoft's documentation lists more than 70 regions at the time of writing (October 2026), and new ones keep opening. Indonesia Central, in Jakarta, opened on May 27, 2025.

A geography is a group of one or more regions that forms a data residency boundary, usually a country or a market such as Indonesia, Japan or Europe. Every region belongs to exactly one geography. This detail matters more than it first appears. Southeast Asia (Singapore) and East Asia (Hong Kong) belong to the Asia Pacific geography, while Indonesia Central belongs to the Indonesia geography. If Nusantara Retail must keep customer data inside Indonesia, the geography is the line it may not cross.

A handful of services are nonregional: you don't deploy them into a region at all. Microsoft Entra ID, Azure DNS, Azure Front Door and Azure Traffic Manager run across many regions, so the loss of one region doesn't take them down. Most of what you build in this course is regional, which means the region decision is one you will make again and again.

Two practical notes before we go deeper. First, not every region is open to every customer: a few regions are restricted to specific scenarios, such as in-country disaster recovery, and need an access request. Second, the same service is not necessarily available in every region, and a new region often starts with a smaller catalogue than an established one. Section 5 shows how to check before you commit.

You will see regions in three places. The portal's region dropdown groups them by geography, so Singapore appears as "(Asia Pacific) Southeast Asia". The Azure CLI command az account list-locations returns every region your subscription can see, with its geography, physical location, paired region and zone details. And Microsoft's region list in the documentation shows the same facts for everyone, including regions you can't access yet.

Scene 2 of 5

Availability zones: separate buildings inside one region

An availability zone is a separate group of one or more datacenters inside a region, with its own independent power, cooling and networking. Zones are placed apart on purpose, typically several kilometres and usually within about 100 kilometres of each other, so a local power cut, fire or flood in one zone should leave the others running. They are also close on purpose: Microsoft aims for round trips between zones of under roughly two milliseconds, which is fast enough to copy data between zones synchronously, before a write is confirmed.

Regions that offer zones have at least three, and a few have more (East US 2 has four at the time of writing). Southeast Asia, East Asia and Indonesia Central each have three. Some regions, mostly older or smaller ones, have no zones at all. And even in a region with zones, an individual service, tier or VM size may not support zones there yet, so always check before you design around them.

Zones protect you from more than disasters. Microsoft aims to roll out platform updates to one zone at a time within a region, so a workload that runs in several zones keeps serving while one zone is being updated. Zones also contain smaller failures, such as a rack or a cluster going down, so the rest of your workload keeps running.

There is one subtlety that surprises almost everyone. When you choose "zone 1", you are choosing a logical zone. Azure maps logical zones to physical zones separately for each subscription, at the moment the subscription is created. Zone 1 in your subscription may therefore be a different building from zone 1 in a colleague's subscription. You can see your own mapping with az account list-locations, which you will do in the lab.

WarningNever assume that "zone 1" means the same building in two different subscriptions. If you split a workload across subscriptions and care about which physical zone each part lands in, check the logical-to-physical zone mapping for both subscriptions first.

TipAt the time of writing, Microsoft's availability zone documentation states that Azure doesn't charge for data transfer between availability zones in the same region. Spreading a workload across zones costs you the extra instances, not the traffic between them. Check the current pricing pages before you put this in a business case.

Scene 3 of 5

Zonal, zone-redundant and nonzonal resources

Having zones in a region doesn't make your resources resilient automatically. What matters is how each resource uses them. Azure services show three behaviours:

  • A zonal resource is pinned to one zone that you choose. A virtual machine and its managed disks created with --zone 1 are zonal. A zonal resource is isolated from problems in other zones, but it shares the fate of its own zone: if zone 1 goes dark, so does the VM.
  • A zone-redundant resource is spread or replicated across several zones by the service itself. Examples are a Standard public IP created as zone-redundant, a storage account that uses zone-redundant storage (ZRS), the frontend of a Standard load balancer, and databases such as Azure SQL Database or Azure Cosmos DB with zone redundancy turned on. When a zone fails, Microsoft fails over to the remaining zones for you.
  • A nonzonal resource, also called a regional resource, is not configured for zones, either because the region has none or because nobody chose any. Azure may place it in any zone and you don't control which one. If that zone has an outage, the resource may go down with it.
Behaviour Who picks the zone Survives the loss of one zone? Examples Good fit
Zonal You (zone 1, 2 or 3) Only if you run copies in other zones yourself VM, managed disk, zonal public IP Several VMs spread over zones; very chatty components you want side by side
Zone-redundant The service, across several zones Yes, Azure handles the failover ZRS storage, zone-redundant public IP, load balancer frontend, zone-redundant databases Shared building blocks that must keep working
Nonzonal (regional) Nobody; Azure places it Not guaranteed Resources created without zone settings; anything in a region without zones Dev and test, or when another layer handles resilience

Storage makes the difference concrete. Locally redundant storage (LRS) keeps three copies of your data inside a single datacenter: cheap, and fine for data you can rebuild, but a datacenter-wide problem can make all three copies unavailable at once. ZRS writes every change to copies in three or more zones before it confirms the write, so a whole zone can disappear without losing data or availability. Lunch 07 covers all the storage redundancy options.

A design that survives the loss of any single zone is called zone-resilient. You get there in one of two ways: use zone-redundant services, or run several zonal instances in different zones behind something that spreads the traffic, such as a load balancer. The second pattern is exactly how you will protect Nusantara Retail's web servers in Lunch 09.

Two mistakes are worth avoiding from day one. The first is copying a script that says --zone 1 for every VM: you end up with a "highly available" pair of servers that both live in the same zone and fail together. Spread zonal resources deliberately, and remember that a zonal VM keeps its managed disks in the same zone. The second is treating zone redundancy as a backup. ZRS protects you from broken hardware and lost buildings, not from people: if someone deletes or overwrites a file, that change is applied to every copy in every zone within moments. Backups, soft delete and versioning (Lunches 17 and 24) cover that risk.

A closer look at Standard public IPs. Public IPs show why you should read defaults carefully. Basic SKU public IPs were retired on September 30, 2025, so every new public IP is Standard. A Standard public IP can be zonal (created with --zone 1) or zone-redundant (created with --zone 1 2 3). What about one created without any zone setting? In a region with availability zones, Microsoft now treats it as zone-redundant: all former nonzonal Standard IPs in zone-enabled regions were made zone-redundant, and an IP that shows zones 1, 2 and 3 behaves the same as one that shows no zones at all. In a region without zones, every public IP is nonzonal. Two more rules complete the picture: an IP's zone setting can't be changed after creation, and zone-redundant IPs can only be created where three zones are live. In the lab you'll create all three variants and compare their zones property. Writing the zones explicitly in your scripts makes your intent obvious to the next person who reads them.

Scene 4 of 5

Region pairs, regions without a pair, and sovereign clouds

Zones protect you inside a region. For the rare event that a whole region is lost, Azure has a second idea: region pairs. Microsoft associates some regions with a partner region, usually in the same geography and far enough away that a single disaster is unlikely to hit both. You can't choose your own pair; Microsoft defines it. Southeast Asia and East Asia are a pair.

If your region has a pair and you use that pair as your secondary region, you get some platform benefits, and it pays to be precise about where they stop:

A region pair gives you A region pair does not give you
Sequential updates: Microsoft staggers planned platform updates across the two regions, so a faulty update is unlikely to hit both at once. Copies of your VMs, databases or files. Nothing is replicated unless you turn on a feature that does it.
Recovery priority: in a rare geography-wide outage, one region of every pair is prioritised for recovery. Automatic failover of your workload. You design, build and test that yourself.
Data residency: almost every pair stays inside one geography. Brazil South, paired with South Central US, is the well-known exception. A choice of partner. The pair is fixed by Microsoft.
A ready-made target for the few services with built-in pair replication, such as geo-redundant storage (GRS). A guarantee that the services you use support pair-based replication at all.

ImportantA region pair is not a backup. Creating a resource in Southeast Asia does not copy it to East Asia. Only features you switch on, such as GRS for a storage account or a geo-replica for a database, move data to another region. Even then, Microsoft-managed failover of GRS accounts happens only in catastrophic situations, so plan and test your own failover whether or not your region has a pair.

Regions without a pair. Many newer regions have no pair at all and rely on availability zones as their main protection. In Asia Pacific these include Indonesia Central, Malaysia West and New Zealand North. "No pair" does not mean "no disaster recovery". It means you choose the second region yourself and set up replication with features that work between any two regions, such as database geo-replicas, storage object replication, Azure Site Recovery for VMs, or backups copied to another region. What disappears are the conveniences that assume a pair: classic geo-redundant storage, for example, replicates to the paired region, so it isn't available where there is no pair. Lunch 17 builds a recovery plan for exactly this situation.

Sovereign clouds. Alongside the global Azure cloud, Microsoft runs separate instances for special legal and regulatory needs: Azure Government for US government agencies and their partners, and Azure operated by 21Vianet in China. They have their own regions, their own sign-in and management endpoints, and often a smaller service catalogue. Nusantara Retail will never need them, but they explain why "Azure" can look different in another market, and why a script written for global Azure may not run there unchanged.

Scene 5 of 5

Choosing a region for Nusantara Retail

Choosing a region is a design decision, not a default you accept in a dropdown. Work through the same six factors every time, and write the answers down so the next engineer understands why:

Factor Question to ask How to check Nusantara Retail
Data residency and compliance Must the data stay in a country or a geography? The region's geography in the region list; your legal or compliance team Customer data should stay in Indonesia, which points to Indonesia Central
Latency Where are the users, and how far away is the region? Test from where your users are; Microsoft also publishes inter-region latency statistics Shoppers are spread across Indonesia, so Jakarta is the closest region
Service and SKU availability Does the region offer every service, tier and VM size you need? The products-by-region page, az provider show and az vm list-skus Check each service first; Southeast Asia has the broadest catalogue nearby
Price Does the same thing cost more or less in this region? The pricing calculator with each candidate region selected Compare Jakarta and Singapore for the SKUs you will really use
Availability zones Can you spread the workload across zones? The region list, and the zone mapping in the CLI Both regions have three zones
Disaster recovery options Is there a pair, and which second region could you use? The region pairs list and each service's reliability guide Indonesia Central has no pair, so the plan is your own

Distances matter for that last row. In Microsoft's published round-trip statistics between regions (median values for the 30 days ending July 30, 2026), Indonesia Central and Southeast Asia are about 16 to 17 milliseconds apart, while Indonesia Central to East Asia is about 47 milliseconds. Those numbers describe traffic inside Microsoft's network; what a shopper in Makassar experiences also depends on their mobile provider, which is why you should measure from where your users really are.

Price deserves the same care. A small difference per hour turns into a real amount for servers that run all month, and a second region adds more than its own resources: replication traffic between regions appears on the bill as data transfer. Price the whole design in the pricing calculator, including the recovery region, before you promise Ibu Sari a number.

For Nusantara Retail the answer is taking shape. Production runs in Indonesia Central, spread across its three zones: zone-redundant services wherever they exist, and several zonal VMs where the design needs VMs. Backups stay in the region. Whether a second copy may live outside Indonesia, with Southeast Asia as the obvious candidate, is a question for the legal team, not for you alone. Meanwhile the labs use Southeast Asia, because it has three zones, a pair and the broadest service catalogue nearby; you can switch any lab to indonesiacentral by setting LOCATION.

Knowledge check

Minutes 45–50
0/5
5 scenario questions. Honest grading.

Answer in your head, reveal, then be honest with yourself.

Q1

Dewi creates the only web server with --zone 2 and nothing in any other zone. Zone 2 then loses power. What happens to the shop, and what is the name for this kind of resource?

The VM is zonal, so it goes down with zone 2 and the shop is offline until the zone recovers. Why: a zonal resource shares the fate of its zone; to survive, run a second VM in another zone behind a load balancer.

Did you get it?
Q2

Product photos are stored in Southeast Asia with zone-redundant storage (ZRS). Are they protected if the entire Southeast Asia region becomes unavailable?

No. ZRS keeps copies in several zones of one region; a region-wide outage needs a copy in another region, for example geo-zone-redundant storage to East Asia or your own replication. Why: zones protect against the loss of a zone, not of a whole region.

Did you get it?
Q3

A colleague says: "We're in Southeast Asia, which is paired with East Asia, so our VMs fail over to Hong Kong automatically." Is that right?

No. A pair gives sequential updates, recovery priority and data residency, but it never copies or fails over your VMs; you would need something like Azure Site Recovery and a tested plan. Why: only features you turn on use the pair.

Did you get it?
Q4

The legal team decides that customer data must stay in Indonesia. Which region do you choose, and what does disaster recovery look like?

Indonesia Central, spread across its three zones, with backups kept in the region; any copy to another region, such as Southeast Asia, would leave Indonesia, so it needs the legal team's approval. Why: the region has no pair, and the geography is the data residency boundary.

Did you get it?
Q5

You create a Standard public IP in Southeast Asia without any zone setting, and az network public-ip show lists no zones. Is the IP zonal, zone-redundant or nonzonal?

Zone-redundant. In regions with availability zones, Standard public IPs created without zones are treated as zone-redundant; only in a region without zones would it be nonzonal. Why: Microsoft made all former nonzonal Standard IPs zone-redundant, so set zones explicitly when you want to make your intent visible.

Did you get it?

Key takeaways

Minutes 50–55

A geography is a data residency boundary, a region is a set of datacenters in one metro area, and an availability zone is a separate group of datacenters with its own power, cooling and network.

Zones only help if your resources use them: zonal resources share their zone's fate, zone-redundant resources survive a zone failure, and nonzonal resources give no guarantee.

Region pairs bring sequential updates, recovery priority and data residency, but never automatic replication or failover of your workloads.

Regions without a pair, such as Indonesia Central, rely on zones; for regional disasters you choose your own second region and set up replication, if your data rules allow it.

Choose a region on residency, latency, service and SKU availability, price, zones and DR options, and verify each one with the documentation or the CLI before you build.

Field notes from StefanusSenior Solutions Engineer · 20+ years in enterprise IT
🗒️

Name the failure before you pick the region. Before anyone opens the region dropdown, I ask one question: which failure must this design survive — a rack, a building or a whole region? Then every box on the architecture diagram gets a label: zonal, zone-redundant or nonzonal. Unlabelled boxes are where the surprises hide, typically a "highly available" pair of VMs that were both created with --zone 1. At the region level my rule is just as short: if nobody can name the feature that copies the data to another region, there is no copy, pair or no pair. And I measure latency from a phone on the kind of mobile network the users really have, never only from Cloud Shell: it runs inside an Azure datacenter, so it can't tell me what a shopper feels.
