🏪 Meanwhile at Nusantara Retail · Episode 01

It is your first Monday as the new cloud engineer at PT Nusantara Retail. Pak Budi, who runs head-office IT, gives you the tour. It ends in a small room next to the pantry: one physical server, a humming fan, and a UPS that beeps whenever the coffee machine starts. That server runs the online storefront for customers of all forty stores. Traffic has tripled this year. The server has not.

At last month's meeting Ibu Sari, the director, asked a simple question: "If that server dies, how long can't we sell?" Nobody answered.

She has given you time to learn Azure properly, with one condition: "No surprise bills. I want to be able to explain every rupiah." Before you design anything, you need a place to practise, the right tools, and guardrails that keep spending predictable.

Today's question: how do you set up an Azure lab where you can experiment freely, without risking the company or the budget?

How this course works

This course is built for people who learn in short, regular bursts. Each lunch is one hour you can protect in your calendar, and every lunch follows the same rhythm, so you always know where you are.

Minutes Segment What you do
5 Scenario Meet today's Nusantara Retail problem and the objectives.
25 Reading Learn the concepts in plain language, with diagrams and tables.
15 Demo Step through an interactive animation; optionally watch a short walkthrough video.
5 Knowledge check Answer five scenario questions. Each answer explains why.
5 Takeaways Review the five key points and preview the lab.
5 Buffer Finish your lunch. Really.

The hands-on lab is deliberately not part of the hour. Most labs take 30 to 90 minutes (the capstones longer), sometimes with waiting time, so you do them later: in the evening, at the weekend, whenever suits you. Every lab is independent. It creates everything it needs, so you can do Lab 17 without ever touching Labs 1 to 16. When a lab needs a starting point, such as two web servers for the load-balancing lab, a setup.sh script builds it for you. Every lab that creates Azure resources also ships a verify.sh that checks your work and a cleanup.sh that removes everything again.

Labs come in two modes. Guided mode explains every step. Challenge mode gives you the requirements only: build it your way, then let verify.sh judge the result.

The course runs over two "months" of weekday lunches:

The core month (Lunches 01–21) covers every fundamental service at the level you need to explain it, choose it and use it. It follows the skills measured for the AZ-900 Azure Fundamentals exam and ends with a capstone that pulls everything together. The deep-dive month (Lunches 22–31) returns to the big topics, such as identity, virtual machines, networking, databases and governance, at the depth an administrator or architect needs. A topic that does not fit into one hour is split into a Basic lunch and an Advanced partner: Lunch 06 (Virtual Machines, Basic) pairs with Lunch 23 (Virtual Machines, Advanced), for example.

Every lab shows a cost band in its header, so you know what to expect before you start:

Band Meaning Typical labs
💲 Free or close to free resource groups, storage, policies, this lab
💲💲 Small charges while resources run, typically cents per hour virtual machines, load balancers, monitoring
💲💲💲 Noticeable charges while running, or slow-to-create gateways VPN gateways, Application Gateway, AKS — delete them straight away

The course never quotes exact prices, because prices change and differ by region. When you want numbers, the Azure pricing calculator gives you current ones; you will use it in Lab 02.

Scene 3 of 6

A subscription you can safely break

To create anything in Azure you need a subscription: a billing and management container that your resources live in. You sign in with an account (a Microsoft account, a GitHub account or a work account), and every subscription trusts one tenant, the Microsoft Entra ID directory that holds users and groups. Lunch 04 explores this hierarchy. For now, remember two things: resources live in subscriptions, and every subscription has someone paying for it.

For this course you want a subscription that is yours alone. There are three common ways to get one:

Option What you get (always check the current offer) Card needed? Good to know
Azure free account US$200 credit for the first 30 days; popular services free for 12 months (for example 750 hours a month of a small B-series VM); 65+ always-free services Yes, for identity verification Spending limit on by default. To continue after 30 days, or once the credit is used, you move to pay-as-you-go. The 12-month free services are for new customers who make that move within 30 days.
Azure for Students US$100 credit to use within 12 months, plus free services No; you verify with your academic email For students at eligible institutions; you can renew every year while you study.
Pay-as-you-go No credit; free monthly amounts of selected services; you pay for usage beyond them Yes No spending limit. Budgets and your cleanup habit are your only brakes.

Read the Azure account options page and the Azure for Students page before you sign up, because offers change.

The spending limit. Credit-based subscriptions, including the free account, come with a spending limit switched on. It is not a number you pick: it equals your credit, and you cannot raise or lower it. You can only keep it or remove it. When your usage exhausts the credit, Azure disables your resources for the rest of the billing period: virtual machines are stopped and deallocated, and storage becomes read-only. Covered services stop costing you money, but they also stop running. The limit has gaps, too: some Azure Marketplace products and support plans are billed separately. And it disappears completely when you move a free account to pay-as-you-go. Pay-as-you-go subscriptions cannot have a spending limit at all.

Which one should you pick? If you are a student, start with Azure for Students. Otherwise, a free account is a comfortable start: the first lunches cost little, and the credit absorbs mistakes. When the 30 days end, you can move to pay-as-you-go and keep the same subscription. Do one thing before that move: create the budget from Lab 01, because the safety net of the spending limit goes away at the same moment. If you have a Visual Studio subscription, it may include a monthly Azure credit for development and testing; check your subscriber benefits. Not sure what you already have? In the portal, open Subscriptions, select the subscription, and look at its offer on the Overview page.

WarningNever do these labs in a company or production subscription, not even "just for a quick test". Your experiments can break shared resources, your costs land on someone else's budget, company policies may block lab steps, and cleanup scripts delete whole resource groups. Use a personal subscription, or one your employer created only for your learning.

Protect the account. An account that can create resources can also create bills. Microsoft now requires multifactor authentication (MFA) for Azure sign-ins: first for the portal, and since 2025 also for the Azure CLI, Azure PowerShell and infrastructure-as-code tools whenever they create, change or delete resources. Set up the Microsoft Authenticator app or a passkey when you are prompted, never share your sign-in, and never commit keys or passwords to Git.

Scene 4 of 6

Three ways in, one front door

You can manage Azure from a web page, from a terminal in your browser, or from your own computer. The four tools below all do the same job; they suit different moments.

Tool What it is Best for Setup
Azure portal The web interface at portal.azure.com Exploring, visual views (costs, metrics, diagrams), one-off tasks None; sign in with a browser
Azure Cloud Shell A browser-based terminal running Bash or PowerShell, already signed in Every lab in this course; quick scripts from any computer None; open it from the portal toolbar or at shell.azure.com
Azure CLI (az) A cross-platform command-line tool Repeatable scripts, Bash users, automation Install on Windows, macOS or Linux, then az login
Azure PowerShell (Az module) PowerShell cmdlets such as New-AzResourceGroup Windows administrators, PowerShell pipelines Install the Az module (PowerShell 7 recommended), then Connect-AzAccount

Azure Cloud Shell deserves a closer look, because every lab uses it. It runs on a temporary Linux host that Microsoft manages, with the Azure CLI, Azure PowerShell, Bicep, Git and many other tools already installed. A session times out after 20 minutes without keyboard activity. The first time you open it, you choose how to treat your files:

  • An ephemeral session needs no storage account. It starts fastest, but everything you saved is deleted when the session ends.
  • Persistent storage mounts an Azure Files share from a storage account in your subscription. Your $HOME directory survives between sessions, and so do your copy of the course repository and your lab suffix. The storage account costs a little, like any storage.

For this course, persistent storage saves you from cloning the repository again every time, but either choice works.

On your own computer, Visual Studio Code with the Bicep, Azure Resources and Azure CLI Tools extensions becomes a comfortable Azure workbench. Lab 01 lists the local tools as optional extras; you never need them for the labs.

What happens when you click Create. Whichever tool you use, the request goes to the same place: Azure Resource Manager (ARM), the deployment and management service for Azure. ARM checks who you are, using a token from Microsoft Entra ID. It checks that you are allowed to perform the action, using Azure role-based access control. It validates the request: is that region name real, does a policy forbid this? Only then does it hand the request to the right resource provider, the service that knows how to build that type of resource, such as Microsoft.Compute for virtual machines or Microsoft.Storage for storage accounts.

Because every tool talks to the same API, a click and a command give the same result. That is why the labs can show a CLI command and a portal path for the same task, and why anything you can click, you can also script.

Which tool when? At Nusantara Retail, the finance team will live in the portal's Cost Management views, because charts beat commands for spotting a trend. Dewi will script her deployments with the Azure CLI, so they can be reviewed and repeated. Pak Budi already has PowerShell scripts for his Windows servers and will feel at home with Azure PowerShell. This course teaches CLI first, because commands are easy to copy, compare and re-run, and it shows the portal path whenever the portal teaches something visual.

One more term you will meet early: a subscription must be registered for a resource provider before it can use that provider's resource types. The portal, and deployments of templates, usually register the common providers for you the first time you need them. If you ever see an error saying a subscription is not registered to use a namespace (for example Microsoft.CloudShell the first time you open Cloud Shell), open Subscriptions › your subscription › Resource providers, find the namespace and select Register.

Scene 5 of 6

Lab conventions and cost guardrails

A few conventions keep the labs predictable, cheap and easy to clean up. They live in labs/common/config.sh, which every lab script loads first.

  • One region. LOCATION defaults to southeastasia. Learners in Indonesia can use indonesiacentral instead: run export LOCATION=indonesiacentral before source ../common/config.sh, and check that each lab's services are offered there.
  • One resource group per lab. A resource group is a container for related resources. Lab NN works in rg-lunchNN-<suffix>, so deleting that single group deletes everything the lab created.
  • Tags. Every lab resource group carries two tags, which are name-value labels: course=azure-fundamentals-lunches and lunch=NN. Cleanup scripts and check-environment.sh find leftovers by these tags.
  • Names. Resources follow the Cloud Adoption Framework abbreviations (vnet- for virtual networks, vm- for virtual machines, st for storage accounts) plus a random suffix of five lowercase letters and digits. Some names, such as those of storage accounts and web apps, must be globally unique; the suffix prevents clashes with other learners. It is generated once and kept in ~/.azlunches-suffix.
  • Small sizes. Labs use small, burstable VM sizes and stay at or below four vCPUs at a time, because free and trial subscriptions have small quotas.

Now the guardrails, the habits that keep Ibu Sari's "no surprise bills" promise:

  1. A budget with alerts. In Cost Management, a budget tracks spending against an amount you choose for a month, quarter or year. You attach alerts at percentages of that amount. This course uses 50%, 80% and 100% of actual cost, and Azure emails you as spending crosses each one. A forecasted alert can warn you earlier, when Azure predicts that you will cross a threshold.
  2. The spending limit, if your subscription has one, as the last line of defence.
  3. Cost bands and small default sizes, so you know what a lab costs before you start.
  4. Auto-shutdown on lab VMs, so a forgotten VM switches itself off at night. You configure it in Lunch 06.
  5. The cleanup habit. Run bash cleanup.sh on the day you finish a lab. Once a week, run bash labs/common/check-environment.sh; it lists any course resource groups that are still alive. Cleanup scripts also remove what lives outside a resource group, such as role assignments, policy assignments or test users, so always prefer them to deleting the group by hand.

Here is how the guardrails play out in a typical month. You finish the virtual machine lab on a Friday evening but forget to run the cleanup script. On Sunday the 50% email arrives. On Monday you run check-environment.sh, which lists rg-lunch06-<suffix> as still alive, and one cleanup.sh later the meter stops. Without the budget, you would have discovered the VM on your invoice weeks later.

TipBudget alerts come from azure-noreply@microsoft.com. Add that address to your safe senders list today, so the one email that matters does not end up in your junk folder.

ImportantA budget is an alarm, not a brake. Crossing 100% sends an email; it does not stop or delete anything. Cost data also takes time to arrive (often 8 to 24 hours, sometimes longer) and budgets are evaluated about once a day, so an alert can arrive many hours after the spending happened. The only things that actually stop spending are the spending limit on credit-based subscriptions, and you, deleting resources.

Scene 6 of 6

How it fits together for Nusantara Retail

Picture the pantry room again. Before you touch the storefront, you now have a safe sandbox: a personal or dedicated subscription, protected by MFA, that holds nothing the company depends on. If it is a free account, the spending limit caps the damage at the credit. A monthly budget with alerts at 50%, 80% and 100% gives you early warnings, and a forecast alert warns you even earlier.

You also have a way of working that suits everyone around you. Ibu Sari gets predictability: labs are tagged, grouped, costed in bands and cleaned up on the same day. Dewi gets speed: Cloud Shell is ready in seconds in any browser, with nothing to install. Pak Budi gets reassurance: nothing in your lab touches his 10.0.0.0/16 network or the company's subscriptions. And when Rizky the contractor arrives in Lunch 05, you will already know that every request, his included, passes through Azure Resource Manager, where identity and permissions are checked.

The director's question is still open. To answer it, you first need the vocabulary of the cloud: what you rent, what you still own, and why paying only for what you use changes the economics of that single server. That is Lunch 02.

Knowledge check

Minutes 45–50
0/5
5 scenario questions. Honest grading.

Answer in your head, reveal, then be honest with yourself.

Q1

Dewi says: "I'll just try the labs in the company's production subscription. I'll delete everything afterwards." What do you tell her?

Use a personal or dedicated lab subscription instead. Why: lab steps and cleanup scripts can break or delete shared resources, the costs land on someone else's budget, and company policies may block lab steps.

Did you get it?
Q2

You created a monthly budget with alerts at 50%, 80% and 100%. You forget a VM, and actual spending reaches 130% of the budget before you notice. What happened to the VM?

Nothing: it is still running and still costing money. Why: budgets only send notifications; they never stop or delete resources.

Did you get it?
Q3

On day 20 your free-account credit runs out and you have not moved to pay-as-you-go. What happens to your virtual machine and your storage account?

The spending limit disables them: the VM is stopped and deallocated, and the storage account becomes read-only. To continue, you move to pay-as-you-go, which removes the limit. Why: the spending limit equals the credit and is designed to stop spending rather than bill you.

Did you get it?
Q4

Pak Budi creates a storage account in the portal. Dewi creates another with az storage account create. Which service receives and checks both requests, and which one actually builds the storage accounts?

Azure Resource Manager receives, authenticates, authorizes and validates both requests; the Microsoft.Storage resource provider builds the accounts. Why: every Azure tool calls the same Resource Manager API.

Did you get it?
Q5

Yesterday you cloned the course repository in Cloud Shell. Today the folder is gone and config.sh has generated a new suffix. What happened, and how do you prevent it?

You were using an ephemeral session, whose files are deleted when it ends. Switch to persistent storage (Cloud Shell Settings › Reset user settings, then mount a storage account), or write your suffix down and restore it with echo <suffix> > ~/.azlunches-suffix after cloning again. Why: only persistent storage keeps $HOME between sessions.

Did you get it?

Key takeaways

Minutes 50–55

A lunch is one hour of scenario, reading, demo and quiz. Labs are independent, done later at your own pace, and always end with verify.sh and cleanup.sh.

Learn in a personal or dedicated lab subscription protected by MFA, never in a company or production subscription.

A spending limit (free account and other credit-based offers) disables resources when the credit is gone; a budget only sends alerts. Pay-as-you-go has no spending limit, so your budget and cleanup habit matter even more there.

The portal, Cloud Shell, the Azure CLI and Azure PowerShell all send requests to Azure Resource Manager, which checks them and hands them to resource providers. The labs use Bash and the Azure CLI in Cloud Shell.

One region, one tagged resource group per lab and a random suffix make cleanup a single command: deleting the group deletes everything inside it.
